Overview: Number Systems

What are Number Systems?

A number system is a method of representing numbers using a base (radix). The base determines how many unique digits are available and how place values work.

Core Concepts: Positional Notation

Understanding Positional Notation

In any number system, the position of a digit determines its value. Each position represents a power of the base.

Formula: (Digit × Base^Position) + (Digit × Base^Position) + ...

Example: Decimal Number 345

345 = 3 × 10² + 4 × 10¹ + 5 × 10⁰

= 3 × 100 + 4 × 10 + 5 × 1

= 300 + 40 + 5

= 345

Example: Binary Number 1101

1101₂ = 1 × 2³ + 1 × 2² + 0 × 2¹ + 1 × 2⁰

= 1 × 8 + 1 × 4 + 0 × 2 + 1 × 1

= 8 + 4 + 0 + 1

= 13₁₀

Binary System (Base 2)

Understanding Binary

Binary uses only two digits: 0 and 1

Every position represents a power of 2, doubling from right to left.

Powers of 2: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024...

Binary Position Chart

Position 8 4 2 1
Power of 2 2³ 2² 2¹ 2⁰
Example: 1101 1 1 0 1
Multiply 1 × 8 1 × 4 0 × 2 1 × 1
= Result 8 + 4 + 0 + 1 = 13

Why Binary in Computing?

Computers use binary because:

  • Easy to represent with electronics (on/off, 1/0)
  • Simple logic operations (AND, OR, NOT)
  • All data is ultimately stored as binary

Octal System (Base 8)

Understanding Octal

Octal uses eight digits: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

Each position represents a power of 8.

Powers of 8: 1, 8, 64, 512, 4096...

Octal Position Chart

Position 512 64 8 1
Power of 8 8³ 8² 8¹ 8⁰
Example: 1567 1 5 6 7
Multiply 1 × 512 5 × 64 6 × 8 7 × 1
= Result 512 + 320 + 48 + 7 = 887₁₀

Hexadecimal uses sixteen digits: 0-9 and A-F

Powers of 16: 1, 16, 256, 4096, 65536...

The Repeated Division Method

How to Convert Decimal to ANY Base

To convert a normal Decimal number into Binary, Octal, or Hexadecimal, we use the Repeated Division Method. This method works the exact same way for every target base!

The 4 Core Steps:

Step 1: Set up a division ladder. Divide your decimal number by the target base (2, 8, or 16).
Step 2: Write the quotient (the whole number result) underneath.
Step 3: Write the Remainder out to the side. (This is the most important part!)
Step 4: Repeat until the quotient reaches 0. Finally, read the remainders from BOTTOM to TOP.

Other Conversion Methods

Method 1: ANY Base to Decimal (Using Positional Notation)

Steps:

Step 1: List each digit with its power of the base
Step 2: Convert each digit to decimal
Step 3: Multiply digit by its power value
Step 4: Add all the results
Example: Convert 10110₂ to Decimal

Position: 4 3 2 1 0

Number: 1 0 1 1 0

Power: 2⁴ 2³ 2² 2¹ 2⁰

= (1 × 16) + (0 × 8) + (1 × 4) + (1 × 2) + (0 × 1)

= 16 + 0 + 4 + 2 + 0

= 22₁₀

Example: Convert 1A3₁₆ to Decimal

Position: 2 1 0

Number: 1 A 3

Decimal: 1 10 3

Power: 16² 16¹ 16⁰

= (1 × 256) + (10 × 16) + (3 × 1)

= 256 + 160 + 3

= 419₁₀

Method 2: Binary ↔ Octal (Direct Conversion)

Binary to Octal:

Step 1: Group binary digits into 3s from the right
Step 2: Convert each group to its octal digit
Example: Convert 111101101₂ to Octal

Group: 111 | 101 | 101

Octal: 7 | 5 | 5

= 755₈

Method 3: Binary ↔ Hexadecimal (Direct Conversion)

Binary to Hexadecimal:

Step 1: Group binary digits into 4s from the right
Step 2: Convert each group to its hex digit
Example: Convert 11110101₂ to Hexadecimal

Group: 1111 | 0101

Hex: F | 5

= F5₁₆
